Uneven gauges

The DCS gauges whose marks are not evenly spaced, each with the conversions rows that turn what DCS-BIOS sends into what the dial reads. Copy a gauge's rows into its reading.

This is parsed data, not flown. Every row here is worked out from the gauge tables in each module's files and the limits in DCS-BIOS, by a script. It has not been checked against each needle in the cockpit.

Expect to adjust it when you set a gauge up: use Learn with the needle on a mark and fix the row that is off. A DCS module update or a new DCS-BIOS can move any of these numbers without notice.

How to read it

From DCS's table to rows

Each module lists its gauges as pairs: a value, and where the needle points for it, with straight lines in between. DCS-BIOS sends that needle position as 0 to 65535, spread over limits lo to hi from its own module file: 

raw = round((needle - lo) / (hi - lo) × 65535)

So a gauge whose DCS-BIOS limits are {-1, 1} puts a needle at 0 on raw 32768, not raw 0. The table shows each gauge's breakpoints as raw → reads, and Copy rows gives the same breakpoints as conversions rows, one per section.

Straight line off by is how far a plain reads from one end to the other would be wrong at its worst mark, as a share of the whole dial. Under 5% you may not need rows at all.

Where a note says the first or last row is the needle's rest, that row is the needle lying off the scale with the power off or the tank dry. Give it the same reads at both ends if you want the screen to hold the scale's end instead.

Reads are in the dial's own units where the unit is known. Where DCS works in m/s, radians or °C and the dial does not, the reads are already converted and the note says so. A gauge marked DCS units reads in DCS's numbers, which may not match the dial's marks.
